JUDGMENT

1. The Claimant, Mr Adam Carter, was injured on 3 March 2025 when a pallet fell from a
lorry operated by the Defendant. He claims damages in negligence.

2. The general principle that a person owes a duty to take reasonable care to avoid
injuring those whom he can reasonably foresee would be affected by his acts derives
from Donoghue v Stevenson [1932] AC 562.

3. In Caparo Industries plc v Dickman [1990] 2 AC 605 the House of Lords considered the
approach to novel situations. In Robinson v Chief Constable of West Yorkshire Police
[2018] UKSC 4, the Supreme Court explained that where a duty of care is
already established by authority, there is no need to apply the Caparo approach afresh.

4. A carrier's duty to take reasonable care that its load does not injure people nearby
is well established. The Defendant accepts that it owed the Claimant a duty of care.

5. The remaining issues are breach, causation and quantum. Mrs Eve Carter, the
Claimant's wife, gave evidence that he was unable to work for six months.

6. For the reasons given below, I find that the Defendant was in breach of duty and
that the breach caused the Claimant's injury.
